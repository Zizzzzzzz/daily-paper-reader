## Abstract 

Tiny UAV detection from an onboard event camera is difficult when the observer and target move at the same time. In this motion-on-motion regime, ego-motion ac-tivates background edges across buildings, vegetation, and horizon structures, while the UAV may appear as a sparse event cluster. To explore this practical problem, we present M 2E-UAV, a benchmark and analysis setup for onboard motion-on-motion event-based tiny UAV detection. The processed M 2E-UAV benchmark contains 87,223 training samples and 21,395 validation samples across four scene families: sunny building-forest, sunny farm-village, sunset building-forest, and sunset farm-village. We provide M 2E-Point, a point-based event baseline, and M 2E-Point + IMU, an IMU-conditioned variant, to analyze the role of inertial cues under on-board motion-on-motion detection. M 2E-Point encodes events as [x, y, t, p ] point sets, extracts local event structure with EdgeConv, and predicts event-level UAV foreground scores, from which bounding boxes are derived via DBSCAN. Our validation-stage analysis shows that point-based event modeling is a strong baseline, while simple IMU conditioning provides only marginal aggregate gains. Under the train/validation split, M 2E-Point achieves 0.9673 F1 and 0.5501 mAP50-95, while the IMU-conditioned variant reaches 0.5561 mAP50-95 with only marginal aggre-gate changes, serving as an initial baseline for future exploration in this domain. Code will be ready in https://github.com/Wickyan/M2E-UAV. 

## 1 Introduction 

Tiny UAV detection is important for safety, inspection, and collision avoidance, but it remains difficult for onboard perception. Conventional RGB or frame cameras can be affected by fast motion, motion blur, illumination changes, and the limited spatial footprint of distant UAVs. Event cameras are attractive for this regime because they respond asynchronously to brightness changes and capture high-temporal-resolution motion cues [ 3 ]. However, onboard motion-on-motion tiny UAV detection is harder than static-observer detection: the sensing platform and target UAV both move, and ego-motion activates cluttered background edges across buildings, vegetation, and horizon structures. Existing event-based UAV, flying-object, and tracking datasets are related to this setting, but they do not fully cover the target problem. Our contributions are: • We define onboard motion-on-motion event-based tiny UAV detection and construct M 2E-UAV, a 720p synchronized event-IMU benchmark covering four scene families with point-level/event-level UAV foreground supervision derived from temporally propagated 10 Hz bounding-box annotations. • We establish a unified validation protocol and evaluate representative event-frame, event-tensor, and event-point baselines, enabling an initial comparison of event representations for onboard tiny UAV detection under observer motion. • We provide M 2E-Point and M 2E-Point + IMU as initial point-based baselines. Our anal-ysis shows that point-based event modeling is a strong starting point, while simple IMU conditioning yields only marginal aggregate gains, motivating future motion-aware fusion. 

## 2 Related Work 

Event-based object detection. Event-based object detection has been studied through several input representations. A common direction converts events into frame-like or voxel-like tensors and then applies image-style or video-style detection backbones. Recent event-native detectors 2further process event streams with recurrent or transformer-based architectures, such as RVT [ 4] and SAST [ 10 ]. These methods show that event tensors can support object detection under high-speed and high-dynamic-range conditions. However, onboard motion-on-motion tiny UAV detection presents a different difficulty: ego-motion can activate dense background edges, while the target UAV may appear as only a sparse event cluster. This motivates our benchmark comparison across accumulated event frames, native event tensors, and event point sets. 

Event-based UAV and flying-object benchmarks. Several recent datasets study UAVs, drones, flying objects, or event-based tracking. F-UAV-D targets fast UAV detection using dynamic vision sensors and embedded inference [ 9]. NeRDD introduces synchronized RGB-event drone detection data [ 7], and FRED provides a large RGB-event drone dataset for drone perception [ 8 ]. EV-Flying studies in-the-wild recognition of flying objects using event cameras [ 6 ]. EV-UAV focuses on event-based tiny object detection and provides event-level annotations for anti-UAV scenarios [ 2]. In addition, event-based tracking benchmarks such as VisEvent [ 15 ], EventVOT [ 16 ], and CRSOT [ 18 ]provide related perspectives on event or RGB-event tracking. These datasets are complementary to our work, but they do not jointly cover the M 2E-UAV setting: onboard observer motion, synchronized event-IMU sensing, 1280 ×720 event data, tiny UAV targets, and point-level/event-level supervision derived from temporally propagated 10 Hz bounding boxes. 

Point-based event modeling. Point-set neural networks provide a natural tool for sparse geometric data. DGCNN introduces EdgeConv to capture local geometric relationships in point clouds [ 17 ], while KPConv [ 13 ] and RandLA-Net [ 5] provide representative point-cloud segmentation architec-tures. COSeg [ 1 ] is also included as a point-set baseline in our benchmark protocol. Although these methods were not originally designed for onboard event-based tiny UAV detection, they provide useful modeling primitives for event packets represented as [x, y, t, p ] point sets. In M 2E-UAV, the target UAV often appears as a small sparse event structure, making point-based event modeling a strong baseline to evaluate. 

Event-IMU fusion and motion cues. IMU measurements can provide auxiliary motion information for onboard perception, especially when the sensor platform is moving. In M 2E-UAV, IMU is included as a synchronized modality rather than as the central claimed contribution. In this paper, the IMU branch is kept as a simple FiLM-style conditioned variant of M 2E-Point, where inertial measurements modulate the event representation through feature-wise affine parameters [ 11 ]. Our validation-stage analysis shows that this simple IMU conditioning gives only marginal aggregate gains, suggesting that stronger motion-aware event-IMU fusion remains an open direction. 

## 3 Benchmark Setup 

3.1 Hardware platform and synchronization 

M2E-UAV is built around an onboard sensing platform for motion-on-motion tiny UAV detection. The sensing platform contains an onboard event camera and an IMU mounted on the carrier UAV. Both the event camera and IMU are mounted underneath the carrier UAV and connected to the platform through a vibration-damping plate. To ensure temporal alignment between the asynchronous event stream and inertial measurements, the two sensors are synchronized through a dedicated STM32-based trigger synchronization board. The processed benchmark is therefore organized as synchronized event-IMU packet pairs, which is important for studying onboard motion-on-motion detection where observer motion directly affects the event stream. The detection task uses only event and IMU modalities, and the paired IMU stream is treated as a synchronized auxiliary modality for analysis rather than as the central claimed innovation.

3.4 Annotation pipeline 

We generate event-level supervision from sparse frame annotations. First, 2D UAV bounding boxes are annotated at 10 Hz. The sparse frame boxes are then propagated between adjacent labeled frames through temporal propagation and temporal interpolation, and this label propagation forms spatiotemporal supervision. Similar to event-level label generation from box annotations in EV-UAV [ 2], the propagated spatiotemporal supervision is lifted to the corresponding event packets through spatiotemporal lifting. This event-level label generation produces point-level/event-level UAV foreground labels. The resulting labels support event-level foreground scoring. Detection-style boxes can later be derived from predicted foreground events during evaluation: foreground events are selected by a probability threshold of 0.5, clustered using DBSCAN with ϵ = 15 .0 pixels and min _samples = 3 , and the largest valid cluster is converted to a bounding box. These AP values are detection-style metrics derived from event-level predictions. They support the present evaluation protocol, but they should not be read as native box-detector results or held-out benchmark results.

## 4 Method 

4.1 Overview 

M2E-Point is the main point-based baseline developed for M 2E-UAV. It keeps each event packet as a sampled [x, y, t, p ] point set, extracts local event geometry with EdgeConv, predicts event-level UAV foreground scores, and converts the predicted foreground cluster into a detection-style box using DBSCAN. M 2E-Point + IMU is an optional analysis variant that uses synchronized IMU measurements to modulate the global event feature through lightweight FiLM conditioning. The design is motivated by a representation mismatch in onboard motion-on-motion event packets. Ego-motion activates dense structured background events along buildings, vegetation, and horizon boundaries, whereas the tiny UAV often appears as a sparse local event cluster. M 2E-Point therefore preserves sparse event evidence and models local point geometry directly, while treating IMU as an auxiliary analysis cue rather than the central contribution. 4.3 Packet sampling strategy 

Each packet is converted to a fixed-size point set of 8192 sampled events. The sampler prioritizes positive UAV events whenever they are available, and background events fill the remaining budget. Training uses random sampling, while validation uses deterministic sampling with a fixed seed. This foreground-preserving sampling rule prevents rare UAV events from disappearing during fixed-size point sampling. 

4.4 Local event encoder 

The event branch encodes local event geometry with two EdgeConv blocks [ 17 ]. Given event features, EdgeConv constructs local graph features from an event and its neighbors, applies a shared MLP, and aggregates neighborhood responses by max pooling. EdgeConv models local event geometry because neighboring events around a tiny UAV can form a compact sparse structure even when the full packet is dominated by ego-motion-induced background events. The first EdgeConv block maps the 4-D event input to 64 channels, and the second maps 64 channels to 128 channels:

## 5 Experiments 

5.1 Reporting protocol 

This submission uses the train/validation split described in Section 3.3 and Table 1. The M 2E-Point baselines are trained with AdamW, cosine annealing, batch size 16, 8,192 sampled events per packet, and 50 epochs; additional implementation and compute details are provided in Appendix B. The checkpoint is selected according to validation performance. Under this split, the validation set serves two purposes: checkpoint selection and validation-stage reporting. We report validation-split results and leave held-out test evaluation to a separate benchmark release. The following benchmark and variant tables report validation-split evidence under a unified protocol.

The lower row zooms into the tiny-UAV region to show sparse target evidence and nearby clutter. 96 Conclusion 

We presented M 2E-UAV, a large synchronized event-IMU benchmark for onboard motion-on-motion event-based tiny UAV detection, with event-level UAV supervision across four scene families. To-gether with M 2E-Point and M 2E-Point + IMU, the validation split establishes a reference protocol for future event-only and event-IMU comparisons. 

Limitations. M2E-UAV currently focuses on four representative illumination-background regimes and uses a validation-stage protocol to establish the first reference results for onboard motion-on-motion event-based tiny UAV detection. Future releases can further expand the benchmark with more weather, altitude, distance, and target-motion conditions.

## B Training and Evaluation Details 

Training uses batch size 16, 8,192 sampled events, AdamW with learning rate 10 −3, cosine annealing, and 50 epochs. During validation, the dataloader uses deterministic sampling with a fixed seed. All reported baseline experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. Training M 2E-Point + IMU for 50 epochs takes approximately 20 hours.
